# Harvest Pool business plan

**Better markets. Stronger harvests.**

## 1. Executive summary

Harvest Pool connects smallholder growers to commercial buyers, combines multiple consented lots into one order, and offers optional packaging and branding quotes. The first pilot would focus on Vhembe District and expand across Limpopo once repeat purchasing, fulfilment and unit economics are demonstrated.

## 2. Problem

A grower with 350 kg of potatoes cannot independently fulfil a 1,000 kg order. Buyers must coordinate fragmented stock, verify availability and align grades and delivery dates. Growers must weigh the price, transport and packing costs before agreeing. Language differences and buyer presentation requirements add friction.

## 3. Solution

Buyers post volume, target price and deadline. Harvest Pool proposes compatible lots and each farmer decides whether to accept. Accepted offers reserve stock. One order records the contribution and value due to each grower. A farmer can separately request a quote for packing, labels or new artwork. Harvest Guide helps people navigate and ask questions in their preferred language.

## 4. Target customers

Start with smallholder potato and other practical seasonal crop growers, grower groups and cooperatives around Vhembe. Potential demand comes from independent fresh produce buyers, retailers, restaurants and institutional procurement teams that need recurring volume. Begin with buyers whose grade, delivery and pack-size requirements can be defined clearly.

## 5. Farmer value proposition

Reach buyers with larger orders, see an offer before agreeing, and keep control over participation. Growers can estimate transport and packaging costs and view their order allocation. Packing can be requested when a buyer?s requirements or a realistic price premium justify it.

## 6. Buyer value proposition

Submit one request and track accepted kilograms across growers. A reconciled breakdown supports quantity and value checks. Potential packaging partners can prepare consistent pack sizes and labels when capacity, requirements and price are confirmed.

## 7. Packaging and branding value proposition

Offer packing in bags, cartons or punnets, printed labels per retail unit and one-time artwork setup. Reuse artwork when appropriate. Confirm food-contact materials, grade, labelling, minimum batches and handover before committing capacity. The pilot assumes partner fulfilment and has no confirmed packhouse arrangements.

## 8. Business Model Canvas

| Area | Pilot hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Customer segments | Vhembe growers and cooperatives, local bulk buyers and retailers |
| Value propositions | Consented pooled supply, traceable allocations, optional retail-ready presentation |
| Channels | Cooperative meetings, buyer outreach, field onboarding and repeat-order referrals |
| Relationships | Assisted onboarding, named order coordinator, issue and re-order follow-up |
| Revenue | Buyer coordination fee, packaging by kg, labels by unit, setup per new design |
| Key activities | Seller reviews, lot matching, quality specifications, handover and reconciliation |
| Key resources | Flask platform, grower and buyer networks, coordinator time and operating playbooks |
| Potential partners | Grower groups, independent packhouses, label printers and suitable transport operators |
| Costs | People, packing and labels, transport, payment fees, insurance and infrastructure |

## 9. Revenue and pricing

Proposed pilot rates are 4% buyer-paid coordination, R2.50 per kg packed, R1 per labelled unit and R150 per new artwork design. Coordination is modelled separately from grower proceeds and excluded from Paystack test checkout. A service request produces an indicative quote. Future hypotheses include procurement and farmer subscriptions, logistics coordination, enterprise contracts and business-support services.

## 10. Competitive alternatives

Farm-gate deals and messaging groups offer direct relationships and low onboarding effort. Existing aggregators and wholesale markets offer established demand and logistics. Generic storefronts simplify discovery. Harvest Pool combines auditable farmer consent and lot-level order economics with optional commercial presentation. Validate the value of that combination with real repeat orders and fulfilment interviews.

## 11. Go-to-market

Recruit growers and buyers through direct outreach and cooperative meetings. Start with one compatible crop and buyer specification. Run assisted requests before scaling self-service. Interview buyers on rejected lots, acceptable pack formats and willingness to pay. Compare quoted pricing with invoices from potential service suppliers before offering the service commercially.

## 12. Operations and fulfilment

Confirm buyer specifications and receiving windows. Review seller identity, contact and authority off platform, then record the decision. Obtain lot-by-lot consent and reserve accepted stock. Confirm consolidation point, quality checkpoints, packing partner capacity, lot identification and handover. Agree rejected-lot handling and replacements before collection. Reconcile order totals and partner charges against completed handover records during the operational pilot.

## 13. Key resources and potential partnerships

A local field coordinator, farm onboarding process, order and dispute procedures, produce specification templates, reliable connectivity and access to food-compliant packing and transport capacity. Approach cooperatives, packhouses and printers for capacity and pricing trials. These are prospective relationships.

## 14. Technology

Flask, SQLite, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, role-protected workflows, seller decision audits, uploaded photo validation and idempotent Paystack test verification. Harvest Guide uses a server-side API when configured and includes a short English and Tshivenda offline platform guide. Move to managed infrastructure and a transactional multi-user database when concurrency and continuity requirements justify it.

## 15. Unit economics

A 1,000 kg order at R15/kg produces R15,000 in produce GMV. At full packaging and branding uptake and one new design, service revenue is R4,250 and illustrative direct service production costs are R2,110. Contribution before processing, logistics, other operating costs and tax is R2,140. Contribution declines with fewer optional services or expensive deliveries. See [Financial model](FINANCIAL_MODEL.md).

## 16. Projections

The editable planning dashboard compares conservative, base and growth assumptions. At starter inputs, the base case models 20 orders per month, R300,000 GMV, R22,550 service revenue and a monthly operating loss of R7,896. The growth case projects operating profit with negative cumulative cash over the first year at its default collection lag. These projections are hypotheses for testing.

## 17. Key performance indicators

Track seller activation and approval turnaround, available kilos, approval rate, time to full pool, order fill rate and repeat buying. Measure quality rejection and late-delivery rates, service take-up, quote conversion, contribution per order, cash conversion and dispute rates. Report data volumes alongside percentages.

## 18. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Pilot response |
|---|---|
| Uneven supply or buyer concentration | Recruit growers across zones and validate several repeat buyers |
| Quality and quantity shortfalls | Use written specs, intake checks and contingency sourcing |
| Packhouse capacity and adverse margins | Confirm minimum runs, test small batches and reprice when bids arrive |
| Slow buyer collections | Set available working capital and credit limits before promising delivery |
| Identity fraud or payment disputes | Verify sellers independently, restrict sensitive records and reconcile exact totals |
| Unreliable translation | Show language and connection states, validate translations with local speakers and keep consent human-controlled |

## 19. Regulatory and compliance considerations

Define the legal intermediary relationship, buyer and grower obligations, dispute handling and insurance with professional advice. Confirm POPIA obligations for account data, external processing, retention and access. Confirm applicable food-premises, transport, lot-tracing and labelling obligations with local environmental health officers and partners. Obtain tax advice on registration, VAT and the treatment of service fees and pass-through payments. [POPIA](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act) ? [Department of Health food control](https://www.health.gov.za/food-control-regulations/) ? [SARS VAT](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/value-added-tax/)

## 20. Growth strategy

Expand after the pilot meets repeat-buying, fulfilment and contribution targets. Add crops and neighbouring districts around verified capacity, common pack specifications and trusted partners. Automate frequent administrative tasks as demand grows. Evaluate subscription, logistics and enterprise services only where recurring usage and buyer willingness to pay support them.
