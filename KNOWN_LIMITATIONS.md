# Known limitations

- **Provider credentials and verification.** Paystack initialization, duplicate checkout, verification, ownership and settlement reconciliation were exercised with mocked provider responses. Live hosted Paystack test checkout and live OpenAI responses require fresh credentials and rehearsal. The earlier credentials should be revoked provider-side.
- **Language.** The built-in English, Tshivenda and isiZulu guide covers platform topics such as pooling, registration, checkout and packaging. The configurable AI integration accepts 11 spoken South African languages. Output quality, including Tshivenda fluency, requires evaluation with speakers. Microphone input uses the browser speech service for the selected language. Read-aloud requires a matching installed language voice. Availability varies by browser and device.
- **Calls.** WebRTC prototype files are dormant. The application has no participant-authorized call route or enabled call interface. A future release requires relationship-based signalling authorization, HTTPS, and connectivity testing with TURN where needed.
- **Seller review.** Approval is a manually audited administrator decision. Production identity procedures, physical inspections and food compliance depend on pilot operating arrangements. Designated test sellers use the isolated demo flag and a separate attestation.
- **Payments.** Hosted payments use Paystack test mode. Settlement allocations are test accounting records and do not represent escrow or payouts. Coordination fees remain illustrative and excluded from checkout.
- **Packaging.** Requests and reviewed quotes track commercial intent and planning. Physical capacity, partners, labelling obligations and delivery need confirmation. The before/after illustration and the AI-generated product mock-up depict sample packaging.
- **Finance.** Projections are illustrative and assume a simplified operating cash model. Actual costs, collections, taxes, VAT, balances and financing are outside the model until supplied and confirmed.
- **Production operations.** Local SQLite, in-memory rate limiting and development Flask are suitable for a single local demo. A public rollout requires production infrastructure, continuous backups, shared rate limiting and operational security review.
